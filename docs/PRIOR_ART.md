# Prior art and novelty assessment

**Review draft — 10 November 2026 (IST).** Companion to [technical audit](RESEARCH_AUDIT.md) and [research proposal](RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md). This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review or proof of novelty. References were checked through live web search and primary publication/documentation pages during this review. No secondary blog is used as technical evidence.

## 1. Search scope, verification and limits

Queries covered dialogue state tracking/correction, multi-intent SLU, Indian code-switching, spoken task-oriented dialogue, confirmation/fulfillment, execution monitoring, durable workflow/idempotency, and recent voice-agent benchmarks. Primary hosts included ACL Anthology, ISCA, NeurIPS proceedings, arXiv, W3C, author-hosted papers, and official vendor documentation. Discovery included 2026 work; relying only on pre-2025 dialogue benchmarks would miss important overlap.

**Verified** below means bibliographic identity/date and the stated mechanism/scope were checked in primary abstracts, publication pages, or documentation. It does not mean experiments were reproduced. Full papers were not exhaustively read for every reference. The τ-Elicitation and Aegis HTML versions and Schneider paper were opened for additional inspection; a section-by-section novelty replication remains future work. AAAI pages for SGD and shielding returned anti-bot denial on direct opening; accessible author arXiv versions were used instead. No paywall circumvention was attempted.

Dates use publication month/year where available; arXiv dates are explicitly first-submission dates, not peer-review dates. Living documentation without a publication date is marked **undated; accessed 2026-11-10**. Repository `main` is mutable; pin a release/commit before experimental reuse. This review does not establish absence of competing Kannada-English correction benchmarks, patents, unpublished work, or proprietary vendor safeguards. Those areas remain unverified.

## 2. Dialogue state tracking and state correction

**[R1] Williams, Raux, Ramachandran and Black. “The Dialog State Tracking Challenge.” SIGDIAL, August 2013.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/W13-4065/). A shared evaluation of tracking a user's state under recognition/understanding errors predates BharatVoice by more than a decade. Maintaining state through noisy dialogue is established work; a new project needs a more specific task or experimental finding.

**[R2] Kim, Yang, Kim and Lee. “Efficient Dialogue State Tracking by Selectively Overwriting Memory.” ACL, July 2020.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.53/). SOM-DST predicts slot operations and selectively overwrites values. Explicit update/carry state operations are prior art. It is a relevant conceptual/implementation baseline for incremental correction, not a ready-made civic complaint model.

**[R3] Xie et al. “Correctable-DST: Mitigating Historical Context Mismatch between Training and Inference for Improved Dialogue State Tracking.” EMNLP, December 2022.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.56/). Detects erroneous or changed slots and generates corrected state to address error propagation. This overlaps strongly with state repair, but model error correction is not identical to grounding a user's explicit self-repair or binding that repair to consent.

**[R4] Ye, Manotumruksa and Yilmaz. “MultiWOZ 2.4: A Multi-Domain Task-Oriented Dialogue Dataset with Essential Annotation Corrections to Improve State Tracking Evaluation.” SIGDIAL, September 2022.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2022.sigdial-1.34/). Revises validation/test state labels while retaining training labels. Annotation quality can substantially change evaluation conclusions. Independent adjudication and a versioned gold set are prerequisites, not optional polish.

**[R5] Rastogi et al. “Towards Scalable Multi-domain Conversational Agents: The Schema-Guided Dialogue Dataset.” AAAI 2020; first arXiv submission 12 September 2019.** [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05855). SGD uses service/intent/slot descriptions and multi-domain dialogue, including unseen-service generalization. A fixed four-category grammar is not evidence of schema scalability. Borrow schema-conditioned baseline design; do not assume the dataset directly supplies approval integrity labels.

## 3. Spoken language understanding and multi-intent extraction

**[R6] Qin, Xu, Che and Liu. “AGIF: An Adaptive Graph-Interactive Framework for Joint Multiple Intent Detection and Slot Filling.” Findings of EMNLP, November 2020.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.163/). Models fine-grained intent/slot interactions across multiple intents. Multi-intent extraction itself is not a new contribution. Its utterance-level setting does not by itself solve identity across repeated instances of the same service.

**[R7] Qin et al. “GL-GIN: Fast and Accurate Non-Autoregressive Model for Joint Multiple Intent Detection and Slot Filling.” ACL-IJCNLP, August 2021.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.15/). Joint multi-intent/slot prediction with graph interactions is an established stronger alternative to splitting at category keywords. Adapting a checkpoint requires schema/data compatibility checks; copying reported scores into a new task would be invalid.

**[R8] Bastianelli, Vanzo, Swietojanski and Rieser. “SLURP: A Spoken Language Understanding Resource Package.” EMNLP, November 2020.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.588/). Provides English speech/semantic resources, baselines, and entity-label evaluation. It motivates separating ASR errors from semantic extraction errors. It is not a code-mixed multi-turn commitment benchmark.

**[R9] Si et al. “SpokenWOZ: A Large-Scale Speech-Text Benchmark for Spoken Task-Oriented Dialogue Agents.” NeurIPS 2023, Datasets and Benchmarks.** [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/7b16688a2b053a1b01474ab5c78ce662-Abstract.html). Human spoken dialogues and cross-turn/reasoning slots establish that speech-text dialogue benchmarks already exist. BharatVoice cannot claim the first speech-aware state benchmark on the basis of a TTS regression.

**[R10] Lee and Pfister. “AVERT: Audio-Verified Adjudication for Spoken Dialogue State Tracking.” arXiv preprint, first submitted 1 September 2026.** [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01828). Combines cross-turn agreement with audio-conditioned verification and restricted state-edit operations. This directly overlaps evidence-sensitive state repair; the proposed project should distinguish transcript-grounded protocol integrity from learning an audio verifier. Peer-review status was not established here.

## 4. Code-mixed Indian-language processing

**[R11] Khanuja et al. “GLUECoS: An Evaluation Benchmark for Code-Switched NLP.” ACL, July 2020.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.329/). Covers English-Hindi and English-Spanish across text tasks. It is evidence for code-switched NLP evaluation, not Kannada-English speech accuracy or spoken number normalization.

**[R12] Diwan et al. “MUCS 2021: Multilingual and Code-Switching ASR Challenges for Low Resource Indian Languages.” Interspeech 2021.** [ISCA primary publication](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/diwan21_interspeech.html), DOI `10.21437/Interspeech.2021-1339`. Includes multilingual Indian ASR and Hindi-English/Bengali-English code-switching tracks. These are not Kannada-English correction-intent labels. Use its distinction between language coverage and actual code-switching evaluation; assess each language/number form independently.

**Implication (inference):** transliterated ASR output can shift number spellings, punctuation and lexical forms, so one English correction clip and several Kannada service words cannot establish mixed-language reliability. A Kannada-English extension needs proficient annotators, recording consent, dialect metadata, and gold semantic/correction labels. No suitable ready-to-use Kannada-English correction-and-consent corpus was verified in this review. This is an unresolved search/annotation need, not proof that none exists.

## 5. Transactional voice agents and confirmation

**[R13] W3C. “Voice Extensible Markup Language (VoiceXML) Version 2.0.” Recommendation, 16 March 2004.** [Dated official specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/2004/REC-voicexml20-20040316/). Form filling, mixed initiative, subdialogs and confirmation patterns are longstanding voice-system engineering. An explicit confirmation button or prompt alone has little novelty.

**[R14] Amazon Web Services. “Confirmation — Amazon Lex V2.” Undated; accessed 2026-11-10.** [Official documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lexv2/latest/dg/intent-confirm.html). Supports confirmation/decline/failure paths and validation hooks before fulfillment. This is concrete industry overlap with BharatVoice's completeness and review gates. Its existence does not establish that every deployment binds consent to the exact last-corrected payload.

**[R15] Temporal. “Temporal Workflow Execution overview.” Undated; accessed 2026-11-10.** [Official documentation](https://docs.temporal.io/workflow-execution). Durable workflow history and replay are established recovery infrastructure. A SQLite save/reload demonstration is not equivalent. External activities still require careful effect/retry semantics; recovering local state does not prove exactly-once downstream behavior.

**[R16] Stripe. “Idempotent requests.” Undated; accessed 2026-11-10.** [Official API documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests). Explicit idempotency keys and retry semantics are established commercial transaction controls. These are design precedents, not a recommendation to put payments in the research scope. A new protocol must compare with ordinary version binding and idempotency rather than omit them as baselines.

## 6. Runtime verification and action safety

**[R17] Fred B. Schneider. “Enforceable Security Policies.” ACM TISSEC 3(1), February 2000, pp. 30–50.** [Author-hosted primary paper](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/fbs/publications/EnfSecPols.pdf). Formalizes execution monitoring through security automata. Blocking forbidden actions with an external monitor is established. A monitor can enforce an observable protocol while the observable language-derived state is wrong; semantic correctness needs separate evidence.

**[R18] Alshiekh et al. “Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding.” AAAI 2018; first arXiv submission 29 August 2017.** [Primary author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611), [proceedings record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11797). A shield constrains or overrides unsafe actions relative to a formal specification. The analogy supports separating uncertain extraction from an action gate; it does not transfer physical-system safety guarantees to natural-language intent.

**[R19] Li, Chen and Wei. “Aegis: Towards Governance, Integrity, and Security of AI Voice Agents.” arXiv preprint, first submitted 7 February 2026.** [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07379). Studies adversarial voice-agent risks using structured scenarios and policy/access controls. “Voice-agent integrity” is already occupied terminology. BharatVoice's initial target should be non-adversarial correction/consent errors, with clearly separated adversarial extensions. Peer-review status was not established here.

## 7. Task-oriented dialogue and agent reliability benchmarks

**[R20] Yao, Shinn, Razavi and Narasimhan. “τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains.” First arXiv submission 17 June 2024.** [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045). Evaluates interactive policy/tool tasks against final database goals and repeated-trial reliability. Final state correctness and repeated-run success are prior art. A proposed integrity score must add temporal correction/approval diagnostics, not rename task success.

**[R21] Barres et al. “τ²-Bench: Evaluating Conversational Agents in a Dual-Control Environment.” First arXiv submission 9 June 2025.** [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982). Extends evaluation to agent/user actions in a shared world with verifiable tasks. Recovery/coordination should be distinguished from merely resuming a saved complaint form.

**[R22] Ray, Dhandhania, Barres and Narasimhan. “τ-Voice: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Voice Agents on Real-World Domains.” First arXiv submission 14 March 2026.** [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686). Adds full-duplex speech, realistic audio, grounded tools and voice/text comparisons. Generic voice reliability, interruption dynamics and speech-to-task evaluation are not open novelty claims.

**[R23] Ray and Barres. “τ-Elicitation: Benchmarking multi-turn entity extraction in voice agents.” First arXiv submission 11 September 2026.** [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13602), [HTML methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13602v1). A 200-task entity-capture benchmark explicitly studies read-back, corrections, confirmation and recovery. This is the closest overlap with the original BharatVoice pitch. A new address-correction benchmark without a distinct scope/consent test would be weak differentiation. Peer-review status was not established here.

**[R24] Sierra Research. Official τ-bench repository, living documentation; accessed 2026-11-10.** [Official repository](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench), [entity-capture documentation](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/main/docs/elicitation.md), [changelog](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md). The current README describes multilingual text/voice evaluation including Hindi, plus entity intake. These are current repository claims, not results reproduced here. Different releases change task/grading conditions; do not compare scores across versions without checking the changelog. Kannada-English coverage was not verified.

**[R25] Ribeiro, Wu, Guestrin and Singh. “Beyond Accuracy: Behavioral Testing of NLP Models with CheckList.” ACL, July 2020.** [Primary publication](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442/). Capability-based minimum-functionality, invariance and directional tests precede this proposal. Correction-preserving punctuation and unrelated-issue invariance tests should credit this testing lineage.

## 9. Defensible gap and publication judgment

**Recommended hypothesis, not established novelty:** an issue-scoped correction-trace benchmark plus a version/evidence-bound approval monitor can identify and reduce stale or wrong-target commitment after self-repair, including cases with identical final slot accuracy. Begin with text and cached ASR, and keep crash replay as a small controlled axis. The contribution is the evaluation construct plus empirical boundary study, not a new number parser or generic safety claim.

Before asserting a gap, inspect R23's scoring and correction policies, R24's entity intake, R10's state-edit restrictions and R19's policy checks at pinned versions. Publish a concrete comparison of label spaces, temporal approval semantics, issue multiplicity and fault coverage. If those already cover the same tests, contribute an extension/replication instead of inventing a new benchmark name.

**Current project:** not a publishable algorithmic contribution; insufficient benchmark scale/independence; a useful demonstration only. **Realistic trajectory:** a focused benchmark/resource or empirical systems workshop paper is plausible after independent data and strong baselines; a full systems paper additionally needs a distinct protocol result and fault/overhead evidence. An algorithmic paper would require a genuinely new method with broad comparisons, which is not supported by the current architecture. Acceptance at any venue is uncertain.
